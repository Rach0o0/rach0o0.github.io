01 — Audio Classification

Environmental sound classification on ESC-50

The goal was to classify 2,000 short environmental sound clips (50 classes — barking dogs, rain, chainsaws, and more) by comparing a convolutional network trained from scratch against transfer learning from a pretrained audio encoder.

My part of the project was the transfer-learning branch: extracting 512-dimensional embeddings from OpenL3 (an encoder pretrained on unlabeled YouTube audio-visual data), and testing how far those embeddings could go. Even a simple logistic regression on top of raw OpenL3 embeddings comfortably beat the from-scratch CNN. I then fine-tuned the last 10 and 15 layers of the encoder, which gave modest but consistent extra accuracy, confirming that pretrained audio representations generalize well even in a low-resource, 50-class setting. The from-scratch CNN — built with asymmetric convolution kernels (wide in frequency, narrow in time) to better capture spectral patterns — still overfit despite pushing training samples from 2,000 to over 14,000 through segmentation and augmentation.

02 — Trajectory Forecasting

Vessel trajectory forecasting & anomaly detection on AIS data

A recurrent model that learns normal vessel movement from AIS (Automatic Identification System) data, then uses its own prediction error as an anomaly score to flag unusual behaviour at sea.

Four conditions, same model

To isolate what actually affects prediction quality, the same single-layer LSTM (128 hidden units) was trained independently on four combinations of season and vessel type, with identical splits, optimizer, and epoch budget.

Training was stable across all four runs — loss dropped smoothly from ~6×10⁻³ to 3.4–3.6×10⁻³ with no overfitting. The clearest pattern wasn't season, but geography and constraint: vessels in narrow, well-defined corridors were far easier to predict than those in open water, where speed and behavioural variance are higher. Fishing vessels, expected to be the harder case due to erratic movement, actually turned out easier overall — most activity stayed within a small, densely-sampled area, and it was the rare isolated trips that produced the largest errors. That residual — the gap between predicted and actual position — is exactly what the model uses as its anomaly score.
